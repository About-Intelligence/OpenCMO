## Core theses (4)

1. **⭐ Pages are cut by "the thing the user wants to make" (JTBD), not by product feature or professional terminology**
2. **⭐ "You can't SEO a terrible experience"** — the SEO landing page must lead straight to the product's magic moment
3. **Internationalization is PSEO's second growth curve** — the same set of templates replicated into dozens of languages
4. **⚠️ The new problem of the AI era: visual-gallery pages are nearly invisible inside LLMs**

---

## ⭐ 1. The most central insight: cut pages by JTBD

**Andre's original judgment, written in 2015** (this is the key to understanding Canva's entire PSEO structure):

> "**Non-designers don't know to search for design principles like alignment, contrast, proximity, etc. They want those design principles applied in context** – say, in designing a logo, or a Facebook cover, or social media graphics."

**→ So Canva's pages are not `/alignment` or `/color-theory`, but `/halloween-poster`, `/resume`, `/flashcards`.**

**His way of working is also worth recording**:
> "When I joined Canva a couple months ago, **instead of writing a word or improving the blog's design, I spent the first month simply building a strategy.**"
>
> "We didn't massively increase the number of fans we have on any social network. We didn't do a major push of our content or sponsor our posts. **The only thing we changed was the way we approached our content.**"

Result (Andre's own first-hand data): blog traffic **+226.47%** in the 60 days after the new strategy.

**Cameron Adams' full description of the method**:
> "He set up a whole team of people who looked at **people's motivations and the top jobs to be done** that Canva could service. He then mapped that through **the entire experience** of going into Google, typing a search query... but then also **the experience after they landed on Canva**."

## ⭐ 2. "You can't SEO a terrible experience" — the thesis of the whole piece

The Halloween poster full journey Cameron Adams describes is the core of the entire strategy:

> Search "want to make a Halloween poster" → Canva is first on Google → click into the Halloween poster landing page → the page tells you how to do it and shows how the product accomplishes it → **one button takes you straight into a Halloween poster template** → a minimal customization onboarding → click done, download the image → **magic moment**

> "**Product led truly means product led because you can't just SEO the hell out of something that is a terrible experience.** So tying that experience at the end of the SEO journey is **just as important as the technicalities of SEO itself**."

**⭐ For anyone doing PSEO this is the single most important point**: the vast majority of PSEO projects die at "the page ranked but the landing experience couldn't catch the user." Canva's pages work because **the SEO page and product activation are one and the same action**.

**In the early days they actually knew nothing about it** (Cameron is very candid):
> "in a couple of our pitch decks, we had SEO as a whole growth channel that we were going to execute upon in order for investors to make a ton of money, **but we pretty much knew nothing about it**. And it was sitting away in our backlog... And we came across Andre and he just really crystallized what SEO was... **It was also incredibly cheap, and it was super easy for us to do ourselves.**"

## 3. Search vs social: Canva's official first-hand controlled comparison

Canva's official blog documents an extremely persuasive controlled pair (**first-hand data**):

Two articles published in July 2015 at the same time, distributed the same way:
| Article | Social performance | Monthly sessions 7 months later (2016-02) |
|------|---------|----------------------------------|
| "How morning rituals boost creativity" | **8,000+ shares** (a social hit) | **525** |
| "Social media icons showcase" | 2,000+ shares | **8,200** |

> "**Publish an article geared for social and it will hopefully implode. But because it's not a topic people search for, it delivers a limited traffic life.** On the flip side, publish an article on a topic that people search for regularly, and that group will continue to find it months after it's published, which gives you an **enduring supply of monthly traffic**. Extrapolate this theory out to 50 or 100 articles a year, and you've got 50 or 100 **self-sustaining articles**."

**The migration of the traffic mix** (first-hand):
| Time | Organic search | Social |
|------|---------|------|
| 2015-02 | 51,000 (12%) | 169,000 (40%) |
| 2016-02 | **434,000 (42%)** | 236,000 (23%) |

(2015-08 was the first month search overtook social)

**A reusable content-audit SOP**: sort every historical article into four buckets — **New / Combine / Keep / Improve** — with the goal of **avoiding keyword cannibalization**.

## 4. Page structure and scale

### Three page types (URL patterns)

| Type | URL pattern | Search intent |
|------|---------|---------|
| **Template pages** | `canva.com/templates/[category]/[sub]` | "discover" (looking for inspiration) |
| **Create pages** | `canva.com/create/[category]` | "do" (hands-on making) |
| **Feature pages** | `canva.com/features/[feature]` | Looking for a specific tool |

**Page structure**: breadcrumb back to the template library → H1 with the category name → filters (style/theme/color/price, **each filter interlinks to a sub-category page**) → thumbnail grid → "create a blank [category]" → footer interlinks to other categories + long text description. Everything is housed inside the `/templates` library; the pages form an **interlinking network**.

**⭐ Splitting pages by search intent** (Startup Voyager's observation): Canva uses **two different pages** to rank for `resume templates` (wanting ready-made templates) and `free resume builder` (wanting to make it themselves) — **it picked up on the subtle difference in intent**.

### ⚠️ Scale and traffic (third-party estimates, differing by an order of magnitude)

| Datum | Value | Estimator |
|------|------|--------|
| English template pages | 24,000+ pages | daydream (based on Ahrefs) |
| `/templates` | ~21,000 pages → 13.1M monthly organic visits | daydream / Ahrefs |
| `/create` | ~2,000 pages → 6.4M | daydream / Ahrefs |
| `/features` | nearly 700 pages → 3.2M | daydream / Ahrefs |
| **Additional contribution from multilingual versions** | **15.5M** | daydream / Ahrefs |
| PSEO total | 38M+ monthly organic visits | daydream |
| Other measurement bases | 30,000+ pages / 190,000+ pages / 400,000+ pages | SEOmatic / practicalprogrammatic / LinkedIn |

> ⚠️ **Page counts range from 24,000 to 400,000 and traffic from 38M to 100M+ — an order-of-magnitude spread**. All are third-party back-calculations via Ahrefs/Semrush, with differing definitions (whether multilingual versions are included, whether subdomains are included). **Any citation must say "as estimated by X" — Canva has never officially published these numbers.**

## ⭐ 5. Internationalization = PSEO's second growth curve

Cameron Adams (first-hand):
> "One of the other pivotal growth moments for us was **internationalization**... We started localizing and internationalizing our products **three years after launch**, which is quite early compared to a lot of other companies... We had a goal of being in **five different languages within the first year**... and we actually hit **eight**. And then we set ourselves a goal of being in **100 different languages** the next year. And the internationalization team smashed that goal by the end of 2017...
>
> **Brazil, India, Indonesia, they're all in our top five markets and they grow way faster than the US does.**"

**→ This is the key amplifier that distinguishes Canva from Zapier**: the same set of template pages replicated into dozens of languages; by estimate, the multilingual versions contribute an additional roughly **15.5M monthly organic visits** (close to half of the English PSEO).

## 6. SEO is an engineering process, not something marketing does alone

Canva engineering blog (**official, first-hand**):
> "The magic of SEO is usually planned upfront and is done by a majority of people in an engineering team including **content researchers, content writers, software developers, software testers, product managers**, and many others. Each role is equally important."

> "Organic results drive a lot of traffic to Canva and are the **second-highest source of traffic**. The main focus is usually on Google but can differ depending on the locale (e.g. **Baidu in China**)."

**Engineering practices you can copy directly**:
- **QA gets involved at the Kickoff stage**: assess indexing/canonical risk before changing a URL; assess Core Web Vitals (LCP/FID/CLS) regressions before adding assets
- **⭐ Test rendering with JS disabled** — "check how a page renders without JS as this is what search engine crawlers would see"; modern crawlers can render JS, but it "**consumes more crawl budget and is not ideal**"
- **Canonical must exist and be self-referencing**; dynamic URLs (`?query=`) point back to the main page
- **hreflang** covers all locales; the main page must have `hreflang="x-default"`
- The Lighthouse SEO score is expected to be 100, but officially they also concede "a perfect score is hard to achieve and **not always necessary as there could be diminishing returns**"
- Run **Test Parties** before launch (team-wide time-boxed exploratory testing)

## ⭐ 7. The AI era: Canva's first-hand stance + a counterintuitive citation paradox

### 7.1 First-hand: treating LLMs as the new Google

Cliff Obrecht (co-founder/COO), live TechCrunch interview:
> "**So we drove a lot of early days in Canva through Google, really understood people's search intent, and delivered them content that they could then go into our product and edit. And so we really see ChatGPT or any of the LLMs top of the funnel acquisition platforms.**"

> "Where we started was… we got the Canva platform, and we're giving the Canva platform a bunch of AI tools. **We're inverting that now. We're becoming an AI platform with a bunch of design tools. So you can think of it more like a cursor for design.**"

TechCrunch's paraphrase: Canva is **allocating resources to show up in LLM search results** while continuing to do SEO; **LLM referral traffic has reached a double-digit percentage**.

Canva's official numbers (self-reported by the company): 265M MAU, 31M paying users, **$4B ARR**; as of 2025-10, users have had **26M+ conversations** with the Canva app on ChatGPT; it claims to be among the top ten most-cited domains on ChatGPT.

### 7.2 ⚠️ The citation paradox: won SEO, lost AI citations

**Third-party analysis** (Foundation Inc, using the Ahrefs AI Index + manual spot checks; ⚠️ the firm is a GEO agency with sales intent):

| Metric | `/create/` pages | `/templates/` pages |
|------|--------------|-----------------|
| AI Overview appearances | **19K** | 2.2K |
| **ChatGPT citations** | **577** | **3** |
| Backlinks | 229K | 269K |

> "The templates section **has more backlinks** than the create section and has earned **three ChatGPT citations. Not three thousand. Three.**"

**Mechanistic explanation** (third-party inference):
> The `/create/` pages carry step-by-step "How to make a resume" sections that **"an AI Overview can lift wholesale"**; the template galleries are **visual assets an LLM cannot extract**.

**→ ⭐ In traditional SEO the template pages win on volume; on the AI-citation dimension it flips completely.**

**Another structural gap**: Canva has **no comparison/alternatives pages of its own** (vs / alternatives). Search "best Canva alternatives" and the AI Overview cites Zapier and competitor blogs; ChatGPT directly recommends Adobe Express, VistaCreate, Microsoft Designer, with **zero citations of Canva**.
> "**A verdict you don't own is a verdict that can be rewritten without your input.**"

**The importance of brand signals** (Ahrefs' study of 75,000 brands): the correlation of **branded anchor text** with AI visibility is **0.527** (AI Overviews) / **0.628** (AI Mode), markedly higher than the 0.218 for raw backlinks.
(Of Canva's 40M monthly organic visits in the US, **roughly 70% are branded terms**)

## 8. Canva vs Zapier: the key differences between two PSEO benchmarks

| Dimension | **Canva** | **Zapier** |
|------|-----------|------------|
| Data source | **Own template library** (the product's outputs themselves) | Integration database (third-party app combinations) |
| Combination logic | Use case × style × **language** | Cartesian product of app A × app B |
| Landing experience | **The page is the product entrance — one click into the editor to complete the JTBD** | The page leads to configuring a Zap |
| Primary amplifier | **Internationalization** (multilingual versions add ~15.5M monthly visits) | Expanding combination count; language is not the main axis |
| The AI era | Template pages get near-zero AI citations; tool pages perform well | Zapier actually occupies the citation slot for "Canva alternatives" |

> **What makes Canva unique**: the "data" behind its PSEO pages is **the product's own outputs**, so the SEO page and product activation are one and the same action. Zapier's pages are "information about the product," with a layer in between.
> This echoes exactly Cameron's line "**you can't just SEO the hell out of something that is a terrible experience**".

## Three directly transferable takeaways (all grounded in first-hand material)

1. **⭐ Cut pages by JTBD, not by feature terminology** — Andre's original insight is "non-designers don't search alignment/contrast; they search logo/Facebook cover." How to transfer: your users don't search industry jargon; they search **the specific thing they want to deliver**.
2. **⭐ The SEO landing page must lead straight to the product's magic moment** — the Halloween poster full journey Cameron describes (search → landing page → one click into a template → edit and download) is the core of the strategy; SEO is only the first half. **The most common cause of death**: traffic arrives, but there is no product and conversion pipeline to catch it, and people come in and leave.
3. **⭐ In the AI era, pages must be extractable** — template pages got 3 ChatGPT citations vs 577 for the create pages, a 192x gap. **Visual-gallery pages are nearly invisible inside LLMs; tool/tutorial pages with step-by-step text are what earn citations**. Meanwhile Canva's missing vs/alternatives pages mean defection queries are entirely lost — when building a site, proactively add these page types.

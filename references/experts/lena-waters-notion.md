## Core thesis: AI isn't a new employee, it's a new teammate — and that changes the entire GTM logic

> "AI isn't your new employee. It's your new teammate and that changes how marketing, product, and go-to-market teams operate at a fundamental level."

Notion's strategy is to **challenge customers to rethink whether their existing workflows should exist at all**, not to bolt AI on as a feature.

## Key insights

### 1. "Show, Don't Tell" compresses the enterprise sales cycle
- Traditional enterprise sales: deck → demo → POC → drawn-out decision
- Notion's approach: **put prospects directly into the product** (rather than presenting it)
- Effect: drastically shorter discovery time; firsthand buyer experience replaces sales persuasion
- Underlying logic: for tool products, the best pitch is usage itself

### 2. PLG and enterprise are one loop, not opposites
- Common market misconception: PLG = low-end, enterprise sales = high-end, kept separate
- Lena's view: individual users enter via PLG → spread within the organization → trigger enterprise purchase
- **PLG is the warm-lead production machine for enterprise sales**, not its competitor
- Different stages of the same user journey; GTM teams need to coordinate rather than operate in silos

### 3. AI workflow redesign: from automating to questioning the process itself
- Layer 1 (where most companies stop): use AI to automate existing processes
- Layer 2 (Notion's goal): **challenge whether the process should exist at all**
- In practice: internal teams can go from idea to launch in **hours** (rather than weeks)
- The mechanism behind it: AI-enabled knowledge sharing that eliminates information silos between teams

### 4. The traditional website may be becoming a growth liability
- AI search (ChatGPT, Perplexity) is reshaping how users discover products
- Users increasingly find tools by "asking AI" rather than "searching Google"
- Traditional landing pages and site architecture may become legacy artifacts
- Implied strategy: content needs to be optimized for AI citation (i.e. GEO), not just for SEO

### 5. Brand: empowerment > control
- Traditional brand management: strict brand guidelines + centralized control
- Lena's view: **letting customers and employees shape your brand** actually produces stronger consistency
- Why: real stories > the company's official narrative; community-carried credibility far exceeds advertising
- Aligns closely with Ben Lang's influencer strategy (authentic usage > reading lines)

### 6. The "Warmth vs. Competency" framework for going upmarket
- The core tension a PLG product faces when entering the enterprise market:
  - Existing users love you for your "warmth" (simple, friendly, human)
  - Enterprise buyers need "competency" signals (security, compliance, SLAs)
- The move: **layer competency on top while preserving warmth**, not either/or
- Failure mode: many PLG companies turn "cold" as they move upmarket and lose the love of their original users

## Proving marketing's value: storytelling vs. attribution data

- Common internal pressure: the CMO must prove marketing ROI, so everything chases attribution data
- Lena's stance: **storytelling matters no less than attribution**
- Why: brand equity, user word of mouth, and community trust are hard for attribution systems to capture, yet they determine long-term growth

## Addendum: Tom Tunguz Office Hours (2026-04-10) — GTM in the agentic era

Source: https://www.tomtunguz.com/lena-waters-office-hours/

### Core thesis: we're no longer selling to humans — we're selling to "tools that report to humans"

> "We used to sell to humans who researched with tools. Now we sell to tools that report to humans."

**Practical implication**: equip your AI agents the way you'd train internal sales champions.

### The two stages of AI transformation

**Stage 1 (where most companies are now): debt repayment**
> "Removing human coordination overhead and calling it transformation? That's debt repayment. It's real value, but it's not a new paradigm."

Using AI agents to stitch together GTM processes that should have been optimized long ago. Valuable, but not a new paradigm.

**Stage 2 (the real paradigm shift)**: redesigning marketing's entire operating logic, not just patching processes.

### The website is becoming a relic

> "Think about how much time we've spent debating the top nav. Solutions before products? Are we allowed to call ourselves a platform? That's applied human psychology. Agents don't care."

- AI agents don't browse websites — they parse content
- Some companies have already abandoned traditional websites for pure Markdown docs (agents' favorite format)
- GTM implication: product information needs to be optimized for agent parseability, not for human visual experience

### How enterprise buying decisions are changing

- Small purchases: agents are becoming the decision-makers (which database to pick, which shoes to buy)
- Large enterprise purchases: agents join the buying committee, but final accountability stays with humans

> "You can't go to your board and say, my agent told me we should do this."

Conclusion: **the more expensive and complex the decision, the heavier the human accountability; the agent's role is research and screening, not the final call**.

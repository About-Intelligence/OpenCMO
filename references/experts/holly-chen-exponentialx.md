## Core thesis: the PLG vs SLG channel choice is determined by ARR stage, not product type

Common misconception: believing "I'm a PLG product, so I shouldn't do sales" or "we sell to enterprises, so we must be SLG."

Holly's framework: **the most efficient channel mix differs by ARR stage; PLG and SLG ultimately converge at the $20M+ stage**.

---

## The PLG vs SLG channel framework (by ARR stage)

### Stage 1: $0 → $1M ARR — find a repeatable path

**Channels that work**:
- Product-led (self-serve signup)
- Founder network and direct sales
- Community and word of mouth

**What not to do**:
- Large-scale paid acquisition (PMF not yet stable; wasted budget)
- Content marketing (takes 6-12 months to show results)
- Partnerships (negotiation-heavy; at small scale you have no leverage)

**Signal to watch**: can you close deals without the founder personally involved in every one? If not → it's not yet time to expand channels.

---

### Stage 2: $1M → $5M ARR — build the initial growth engine

**Channels that work on the PLG path**:
1. **SEO + content marketing**: start building organic traffic assets (SEM data can already validate keywords by now)
2. **In-product viral loops**: invite features, collaboration-triggered sharing
3. **KOL/influencer** (see Ben Lang's Notion model)

**Channels that work on the sales-led path**:
1. **Outbound (data-backed)**: use intent signals rather than random cold email
2. **Small-scale field marketing**: find high-intent audiences

**Common to both**:
- **Referral programs**: the user base is large enough at this stage for incentivized sharing to have reasonable ROI

---

### Stage 3: $5M → $20M ARR — scale what works, don't spread across everything

**Danger traps for PLG products**:
- Starting to believe organic growth can carry you indefinitely, while CAC actually starts climbing
- SEO content quality degrades at scale; rankings become unstable

**Holly's core advice**:
> "At this stage, the question isn't PLG or SLG. It's what's your conversion motion from free to paid, and from paid to enterprise."

Focus shifts to:
- **Product Qualified Lead (PQL) systems**: identify which free users show paid signals and trigger sales involvement
- **B2B influencer marketing** (see the dedicated section below)

---

### Stage 4: $20M ARR+ — PLG and SLG converge

> "Every successful PLG company above $20M ARR starts looking like a SLG company. The question is whether you plan for that or stumble into it."

At this stage nearly every high-growth SaaS will:
- Stand up a dedicated enterprise sales team
- Build a Customer Success function (preventing churn and driving expansion)
- Start executive relationships with major accounts

PLG's unique advantage at this stage shows up as:
- A bottom-up user base already inside the enterprise (warm leads)
- Product validation via "use before you buy" during sales negotiations

## B2B influencer marketing: the emerging highest-ROI channel

Holly is an early advocate of B2B influencer marketing and sees it as the most underrated growth channel of 2024-2026:

### How it differs from B2C influencers

| Dimension | B2C influencer | B2B influencer |
|------|---------------|----------------|
| Platforms | Instagram/TikTok | LinkedIn/Twitter/YouTube |
| Metrics | Followers/engagement rate | Target-audience fit |
| Content format | Showcase/entertainment | Education/opinion |
| Partnership logic | Buying exposure | Co-creating content |
| Typical profile | Mega-influencers | Industry practitioners (1K-50K followers, but a precise audience)|

### Criteria for finding B2B influencers

Not the ones with the most followers, but those who:
1. **Have a precise audience**: followers are your target customers (CFOs/CTOs/PMs/engineers)
2. **Actually use the product**: high overlap with your product's ICP
3. **Have a credible content style**: not a "sponsored-promotion account" but a practitioner with opinions
4. **Are open to long-term partnership**: one-off ads vs. an ongoing content partnership

### Practical playbook

```
Find people → test with small budgets ($500-2000/post) → track UTM conversions
→ identify the 3-5 who perform → sign quarterly deals
→ encourage organic usage and sharing (don't control the content angle)
```

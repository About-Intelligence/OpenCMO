## Core proposition: in the AI era, 60–70% of traditional growth tactics no longer work

> "In the age of AI, somewhere between 60 to 70 percent of traditional growth tactics don't apply anymore."

This is a discontinuity, not a gradual shift. Elena's core argument: AI products and traditional SaaS products differ fundamentally in their growth mechanics, and reusing the old methods only makes teams hit the wall faster.

---

## Key insights

### 1. The PMF Treadmill

Elena's most influential original framework:

**Traditional PMF thinking**: find PMF → hit the gas on growth → run all the way to IPO

**AI-era reality**:
> "PMF is not a destination. It's a treadmill. The moment you think you've found it, the ground shifts again."

Why: AI capability makes an order-of-magnitude jump every 3–6 months, and user expectations reset accordingly. The feature that makes users shout "wow" today becomes a baseline expectation in 6 months, and a burden 6 months after that ("why am I still being asked to do this step?").

**Practical implications**:
- You can't do one PMF study and be done forever
- Growth teams need to re-validate core user behavior signals every quarter
- The baseline for retention metrics keeps rising (the same retention rate that passed 6 months ago may be failing now)

### 2. PLG for AI products: friction is no longer a filtering mechanism

Traditional PLG logic: add friction to the free tier (feature limits, quotas) → users with purchase intent are willing to push through the friction → high-quality SQLs get filtered out naturally.

The problem for AI products: **AI can eliminate the friction itself**. If a competitor's AI can directly accomplish what the user wants to do, any friction is a churn point.

> "The value delivery in AI products is so immediate that the traditional 'aha moment' has collapsed into the first interaction."

Old playbook: design an onboarding funnel, 7 steps guiding the user to the aha moment  
New playbook: the very first prompt/action must deliver value — there is no "onboarding period"

**Supporting data (Phil Carter / RevenueCat)**: in consumer subscription products, the share of trials starting on day zero already exceeds 80%, approaching 90% in some categories — data that confirms Elena's judgment.  
→ Phil Carter's full framework: [experts/phil-carter-elemental-growth.md](phil-carter-elemental-growth.md)

### 3. Three reasons traditional growth tactics fail

| Failure category | Traditional tactic | Why it fails |
|---------|---------|----------|
| **Invite loops** | Invite friends to unlock features | AI products' individual value is too strong, so sharing motivation is weak; and friends can just use a competitor directly |
| **SEO content growth** | Mass-produce SEO articles | AI-generated content floods the web, Google demotes it; users just ask an AI directly instead |
| **Feature-limited freemium** | 5 free / limited to X uses | Competitors offer free and unlimited; limits only accelerate churn |

### 4. Which growth tactics still work in the AI era?

Tactics Elena believes **still work**:
- **Community and word of mouth**: AI product differentiation is hard to convey through ads; real user stories carry further
- **Workflow Embedding**: make the product an un-removable step in the user's daily workflow — stickier than any feature lock-in
- **Data network effects**: usage data makes the product better, forming a moat competitors can't replicate
- **Enterprise motion**: enterprise budgets for AI are noticeably more flexible than for traditional SaaS (the "AI tax" mentality)

### 5. Lovable's experiment: growing a 0→1 AI product

The core of Lovable's (AI-generated web apps) growth model:
- Every app a user produces is a distribution node (the "built with Lovable" watermark)
- Product-led virality: every deployment is a brand impression
- Positioned for non-technical users: reaching a market traditional dev tools never touched at all

### 6. ⚠️ Paid acquisition in year one is a "death trap"

> Pouring money into paid marketing before thoroughly validating PMF and getting the organic growth funnel working is just burning cash.

**Key yardsticks**:
- **Any company under five years old talking about LTV is talking nonsense** — you simply don't know the true lifetime value
- You can only look at the **payback period**
- **⭐ If your conversion cycle is longer than 3 months, don't touch paid acquisition**

**Distribution comes in two kinds** (her core framework for judging moats):

| | Definition | Is it a moat? |
|---|------|------------|
| **Earned distribution** | Word of mouth, community, organic user sharing — built on user trust | ✅ Yes |
| **Paid distribution** | Paid ads, etc. | ❌ No |

> The companies that ultimately win in the market are those with **"earned," "competitively defensible," "sustainable," and "predictable"** distribution channels.

**⚠️ Note**: she is not against paid acquisition itself — asked "how would you spend an unlimited marketing budget," the first thing that came to her mind was large-scale media buying (see section 11). What she opposes is **spending without validation**.

### 7. "Purely functional SaaS" will be beaten by big players' distribution advantage

**When product features are highly commoditized, distribution channels become the key to winning.**

Facing OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Apple, and other giants with massive distribution networks, a startup's product will face an overwhelming assault unless it has **emotional stickiness and an irreplaceable experience**.

**From MVP to MLP (Minimum Lovable Product)**:
> Getting basic functionality working (the safety and usability at the bottom of Maslow's hierarchy) is no longer enough. Software is now being judged by **"the emotions it can evoke."** A product needs "personality" that lets users form an emotional connection — **this is the minimum bar for starting growth**.

**The underlying judgment**: in the AI era, the barrier to writing code and shipping features is dropping toward zero, so features are no longer the core differentiator. **The core of growth has become a "trust problem"** — users buy or use your product because they trust you and your team.

### 8. Metrics: depth of usage can be an inverse indicator

**⭐ For productivity tools, "the longer users stay, the better" is wrong**:
- Social platforms: the longer users stay, the better
- **Productivity tools**: if users are spending a lot of time, it may mean **the product is too complex and users are stuck**
- The optimization direction should be **making the task simpler**
- **Don't blindly chase "time spent" — focus on "frequency"**

**Frequency is life or death**:
> Habits typically form on a cadence of days or weeks. **If your product is a "monthly active" product, you slide easily into users' "forgetting zone."**

**Beware vanity metrics**:
- ❌ Treating "logins" as activity = a meaningless vanity metric
- ✅ Lovable defines active as **"currently building an app" or "a published app receiving traffic"** — the behaviors that actually produce value

---

### 9. Pricing: break the "subscription" fixation

**The problem**: the SaaS industry has been obsessed with ARR, but **AI product usage is often bursty/intermittent** — when inspiration or a project strikes, users use it heavily, then a quiet stretch may follow.

**What to do**: you must offer a flexible **pay-as-you-go / top-up** model.

**⭐ A counterintuitive real-world result**:
> Elena found that adding a top-up option **not only didn't cannibalize subscription revenue — it brought substantial incremental revenue and improved retention**.

**Long-term direction: from "paying per call" to "paying for business outcomes"**
> Many AI companies today are simply passing expensive large-model costs on to users, which is not sustainable. Large-model compute costs will eventually become extremely cheap, like cloud services and internet access. **The eventual winners on business model will be the companies that move first from "pay per model call" to "pay for business outcomes."**

---

### 10. Release cadence is a retention strategy

**Lovable insists on shipping daily**, rather than the traditional monthly/quarterly big release.

**Why**:
> The constant market noise and updates need to make users feel the product is **"a living, breathing, constantly evolving organism."** These continuous micro-vibrations powerfully drive resurrection and retention of existing users and keep the product from being forgotten.

**A tiered release mechanism**:
- **Daily**: engineers directly ship experience-improving updates and speak about them on social media themselves
- **Tier 1**: the marketing team concentrates its firepower, handling only the major releases **once every 1–2 months** and telling the product story well

**Companion: all-hands marketing and going "full-stack"**
- With AI, professional boundaries are blurring; employees need to understand marketing, not just product or code
- Founders and even all engineers are encouraged to "build in public" on social media
- **Employees building personal brands doesn't lead to talent attrition — it turns them into the company's most powerful free marketing nodes**

---

### 11. Community: don't turn it into a "support trash can"

**Common mistake**:
> Many companies build a community only because support can't keep up with the messages, which leaves the community **filled with negativity and bug reports — which then get indexed by search engines**.

**The right way**:
> Early on, find the **fanatically enthusiastic "superusers,"** build the community around them, and let their passion infect and draw in others, keeping the community positive and inspiring.

**On whether SEO is dying because of AI search** (her three-layer judgment):
1. **She concedes traffic is declining, but "total death" is still many years away** — SEO's installed base is enormous
2. **It is still the "baseline" of growth and still needs investment today** — if you want to maintain your company's growth baseline, you **must** invest in SEO
3. **⭐ But it is no longer the "decisive factor" that makes a company stand out and win** — "SEO will absolutely not be the reason your business wins or stands out in the market. It is merely table stakes."

**What she would do with an unlimited marketing budget** (showing she doesn't reject paid):
1. **⭐ Go big on out-of-home advertising again** — she believes **OOH is making a strong comeback and is where you can truly capture mass eyeballs and attention**
   > This corroborates the actual 2024–2026 campaigns of Stytch / Notion / Clay / Ramp → see [channels/out-of-home.md](../channels/out-of-home.md)
   > **⚠️ Note this does not contradict her "paid acquisition in year one is a death trap"**: she opposes throwing money before validating PMF, not paid/brand channels themselves. OOH is a **maturity-stage amplifier** — the same judgment as Reed's "Series B+" threshold and Meltem's "do 6–8 months of bottom-of-funnel first," expressed three different ways
2. **Invest heavily in video and audio streaming ads** — not just pure visuals; go heavy on video and conversational formats (which explain the product more clearly); focus spend on **Amazon Prime Video and Spotify**, where **competition is still relatively light**
3. **"Monopoly-style" investment in the creator economy** — madly sponsor every relevant newsletter and podcast, using overwhelming spend to squeeze competitors out
4. **Turn every customer into a "walking billboard"** — send every customer **top-quality** branded merch (cool enough that they genuinely want to wear it), so customers display the brand in their daily lives

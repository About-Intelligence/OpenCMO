## Core thesis: $35K total marketing budget, $1.25B valuation — growth doesn't need a growth team

> "We've spent maybe $35,000 total on marketing in our entire company history."

This is a complete growth philosophy, not a gimmick: **when the product itself becomes an ideology, users spread it for you**.

## Linear Method: product philosophy as growth flywheel

### The core document

Linear Method is a methodology on "how to build software effectively," published openly at linear.app/method.

Key traits:
- A **belief system**, not "feature documentation"
- Covers: project-management philosophy, what good engineering culture is, how to do roadmaps
- Why users spread it: people who identify with these values adopt Linear as a "we're this kind of people" identity badge

> "We didn't try to convert people. We tried to attract people who already believe what we believe."

### Why methodology can replace marketing

| Traditional growth tactic | Linear's replacement |
|------------|-------------|
| Google Ads | Engineers sharing organically on Twitter |
| Content marketing (SEO) | The Linear Method doc itself gets cited |
| Sales/SDR | Product word of mouth |
| Case studies | Companies publicly using Linear as self-endorsement |

## Key insights

### 1. Zero A/B tests, intuition-led

Karri explicitly rejects A/B-test-driven product decisions:

> "We don't really A/B test. I think A/B testing is often a way to avoid making real decisions."

The logic: if your core product judgments depend on A/B tests, you don't have a sufficiently clear product thesis. A clear thesis is itself a growth engine.

**Counterintuitive**: most "growth best practices" are A/B-test-driven; Linear went completely the other way and succeeded.

### 2. Target market: engineers > project managers

Most project-management tools center on PMs as the core user; Linear flipped it:

- **Primary users: engineers** (not PMs)
- Engineers actually use the tool rather than just viewing it
- Engineers have extremely low tolerance for clunky tools, and once they find one they love, they recommend it loudly

Result: engineers built organic word of mouth on Hacker News and Twitter — Linear's biggest source of users.

### 3. Speed = brand value

The core of Linear's product experience is **speed** (keyboard-driven, millisecond-level response). This is more than a feature:

> "Speed is a feature. But more than that, it's a statement about what we believe software should feel like."

Speed became the brand's core metaphor: Linear = fast, efficient, no nonsense. This metaphor resonates deeply with engineering culture.

### 4. Pricing strategy: per-seat pricing vs. feature tiers

- **Chose per-seat pricing**: engineering teams have headcount, so revenue expands naturally as teams grow
- **No freemium "feature wall"**: paid and free share the same core experience; conversion isn't driven by feature-gating
- Conversion comes from: teams needing collaboration → naturally upgrading to a team plan

### 5. Enterprise motion: bottom-up needs no active push

Linear has no active enterprise sales motion, yet plenty of enterprise teams use it:

- Path: an engineer uses it personally → recommends it to the team → the team becomes an enterprise contract
- No AEs, no SDRs, no demo calls
- Enterprise purchasing happens self-serve (credit card)

## Where it applies vs. its limits

**Applies when**:
- Product quality is exceptionally high, with a clear product thesis
- Target users live in technical communities (engineers, designers) with strong taste and a strong urge to share
- The team is willing to position the product as an "ideology" rather than a feature list

**Doesn't apply when**:
- Consumer products needing rapid scale (organic growth is too slow)
- The product isn't clearly differentiated from competitors
- Target users aren't in strong word-of-mouth communities

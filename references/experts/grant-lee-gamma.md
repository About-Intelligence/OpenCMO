## Core theses (7)

1. **Build a "word-of-mouth machine" before anything else** — if word of mouth isn't a large share of new users, fix the product, don't buy traffic
2. **PMF has two checkpoints**: organic growth pull, then willingness to pay — Product Hunt wins are vanity metrics
3. **The first 30 seconds are the product** — onboarding isn't a flow, it's the lens that tells you what the product should be
4. **Influencer marketing = thousands of micro-influencers, never scripts, founder onboards them personally** — big-name creators are the wrong approach
5. **Paid acquisition must stay a minority channel**: >50% paid = "your core growth engine is broken"
6. **Profitability came from experimentation infrastructure, not pricing genius** — testing 20+ models in production instead of defaulting to the most expensive one
7. **A durable "GPT wrapper" = deep ownership of one end-to-end workflow** you care enough about to spend 5–10 years on

## ⭐ PMF story: winning Product Hunt and still calling it "not working"

August 2022 public beta: won Product Hunt product of the day, week, AND month. Then signups plateaued.

> "We were still getting new users every day, but it was clear we didn't have strong word of mouth. There wasn't strong organic virality. And so if we just kind of played things out, we knew that the product wasn't going to grow on its own."

By late 2022 (per his own blog post): ~60,000 users, a little over a year of runway, 12-person team. The bet-the-company decision:

> "We are going to do everything we possibly can to make the first 30 seconds of the product feel magical. The moment you land into the product, it has to be great, and it has to be so great that someone that goes through that onboarding is going to tell all their friends."

They spent 3–4 months rebuilding onboarding with AI generation inside it, and relaunched end of March 2023:

> "All of a sudden, we'd go from a few hundred signups a day to now first day it was like a couple thousand, and then the next day would be like 5,000 signups, and then 10,000 signups a day, and then 20,000 signups a day... We weren't doing any sort of marketing, no advertising."

The trap he says they avoided:

> "The temptation would've been, hey, let's just spend more on ads or spend more on marketing... I think that would've been a trap. I think that would've led us down to this path of trying to brute force our way into product market fit."

**The launch itself was engineered virality**: a deliberately provocative tweet — "The most valuable skill in business is about to become obsolete." — which Paul Graham criticized in a comment, which drove even more engagement. Grant: "Totally. I'd say provocative. A little spicy."

**Onboarding heuristic**: treat new users as "selfish, vain, and lazy," and throw one egg — "You throw a consumer one egg, they can probably catch it. You throw them four or five eggs, they're probably going to drop all of them."

## ⭐ Influencer marketing: the tactic he's most cited for

From the X post (verbatim): "0-10M was nearly 100% word of mouth and organic content. 10-50M was still >50% word of mouth but influencer, affiliate, and referral made up the other half."

**The playbook** (X post + podcast, first-hand):

- > "90% of your reach comes from <10% of content that goes viral. Your job: go broad with influencers and spend enough to find that 10%."
- Micro over macro: "You're much better doing the hard thing, which is hard to scale, finding the thousands of micro influencers that have an audience where your product maybe is actually useful." Big creators + scripts "immediately feels like an ad."
- > "Never write scripts - it becomes an ad no one watches. Creators know their audience best."
- **Founder does the onboarding**: "All the initial influencers, I onboarded manually myself. I would jump on a call with each one of them... You want to be able to have them tell your story but in their voice."
- Budget floor: "Start with $10-20k/month, commit to 6 months minimum." Started with ~20 creators/month → 50 → 100. Per-creator cost: "few hundred to low single digit thousands" of dollars.
- Don't put eggs in one basket: with $20K, work with 40 influencers across personas, then double down on what works ("If educators are working, go with educators").
- **Echo chambers**: pockets like teachers — "teachers love telling other teachers about products they love using."
- **Measured amplification**: word of mouth is >50% of new subscriber growth even at scale, and "anytime you spend a little bit of money [on influencers]... the word-of-mouth factor actually will get another 1.5 additional users on top of that."
- Channel power law: "LinkedIn, the conversion rates are just substantially higher. They're 4X, maybe 5X higher than other platforms... a lot of people are probably still sleeping on LinkedIn." Twitter was NOT impactful for Gamma. On TikTok, have creators start new accounts.
- Tools/partners he names: 1stCollab (automated creator outreach, YC), AKG Media (UK agency); they open-sourced the entire brand at brand.gamma.app so creators can reuse assets.

## Brand before performance; paid as constrained amplifier

> "Most startups are not prepared to scale their spend because their brand and creative sucks. We had to go through an extremely expensive rebrand... But it would have been much more expensive to skip this step." (X post)

Rebrand happened at ~$10M ARR, with agency Smith & Diction. His test for brand readiness: "If I were to scale this up to a thousand pieces of creative, would it still feel cohesive?"

> "Brand marketing is performance marketing. Everything is some form of performance marketing. It just might not be as attributable."

Two rules before ramping paid:

1. "Don't invest until you have word of mouth."
2. > "Set some constraints. You don't want your product to be at a point where more than 50% of your acquisitions are coming through paid acquisition. I think if that is happening, your core growth engine is broken."

On the CAC treadmill: "If you rely on paid acquisition to be the main growth engine, you should be prepared for things like CAC to keep going up... it's almost impossible to get off that treadmill when you're too far into it."

Growth mix post-$10M ARR (confirmed on the podcast): #1 word of mouth (>50%), #2 influencer/social, #3 performance marketing.

## Experimentation culture: same-day prototype tests + model-cost discipline

- Workflow: "We would have an idea in the morning, come up with some sort of functional prototype... In the afternoon, we're already running pretty full scale experiment... By the evening or by the next day, we can actually go through all of it together." ~20 testers per prototype, recruited via Voicepanel / UserTesting — "legitimately good prospective users, but have zero skin in the game. They do not care at all whether or not your product succeeds" (friends lie to you).
- Applied to "everything: landing pages, onboarding experience, new feature launches, export, sharing."
- Power-user Slack ("Gammaster Program") for early wireframe feedback.
- **Profitability link** — the model-cost angle Phil Carter cites: "We run profitably, we have really strong margins. I don't think that would be possible if experimentation wasn't core infrastructure to us because the temptation would be you just throw the most expensive model at every job and assume that's going to work. And the reality is that never is the case. And so for us to be able to actually test across 20 different models in production today..."
- Founding A/B test: they built two products in parallel for 6 months (a virtual office called "The Lobby" vs. reimagined presentations) and dogfooded both. Kept the one where they "could imagine a product 100x better than the alternative."

## Pricing: forced by users, anchored by ChatGPT, profitable in months

March 2023 relaunch had zero monetization — 400 free credits, then a hard stop. "Intercom for us was just blowing up with people saying, 'How do I purchase more credits?'" April 2023: Van Westendorp + conjoint analysis on early users → single ~$20/month plan (consciously anchored to ChatGPT's price point), shipped end of May 2023.

> "Within a couple of months we had hit a million in ARR and became profitable."

Why so disciplined: "We also didn't have a choice because at that point runway was also low. So if we weren't making money, well we would actually be in a tough spot."

## Durable "GPT wrapper" + tiny team

- Wrapper thesis: "When you think about maybe just literally only being a wrapper on one model or one provider, yeah, maybe there's only limited amount of utility... [instead] go really deep into this one workflow, and it's not just one model. It's maybe 20 plus models powering all different parts of the product." Precondition: "Is this a problem I can invest five to 10 years into actually solving?"
- Team: "Hire painfully slowly." First 10 employees all still there after 5 years. All managers are player-coaches doing IC work; hire generalists; at one point ~25% of the team were product designers. From the blog post: "We chose profitability over vanity metrics... 50 employees instead of 200."

## Counterintuitive takes (most worth remembering)

- **Winning Product Hunt day/week/month ≠ PMF** — signup plateau + weak word of mouth means bet the company anyway
- **>50% paid acquisition = broken growth engine** — a hard numeric ceiling on paid, stated as a rule
- **Never give influencers a script**; the founder personally onboards each early creator on a call
- **You cannot pick winners in influencer marketing** — "I was not a good predictor of that. I just knew that we had to play the sort of numbers game"
- **A rebrand mid-hypergrowth was a growth investment**, justified because thin brand DNA capped how much creative they could test
- **Experimentation across 20+ models is a profitability strategy**, not just a quality strategy — refusing to default to the most expensive model
- **Launch tweet was intentionally provocative** to bait engagement (and it baited Paul Graham)
- **LinkedIn converts 4–5x better than other influencer platforms** for prosumer B2B; Twitter did little for Gamma
- **Hire painfully slowly**; headcount goals corrupt hiring bars

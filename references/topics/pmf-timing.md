# When to Start Running Ads (PMF Timing)

## Core Conclusion

**Ads amplify existing results; they don't create PMF.** Running ads at the wrong time wastes money and, worse, creates a negative feedback loop.

**⚠️ But on "what counts as the right time," four practitioners give four different answers** — the disagreement looks large, but it really comes down to how much of the groundwork each of them counts as part of "running ads." Read the comparison table below first, then each expert's full framework.

---

## ⭐ The four positions compared

| Expert | Position | Preconditions | Track record |
|------|------|---------|---------|
| [Matt Swulinski](../experts/matt-swulinski-wispr-flow.md) (Wispr Flow) | **Start immediately** | Conversion tracking working + **accumulate 50 conversions** | ARR $2M→$50M in 12 months |
| [Rex Gelb](../experts/rex-gelb-cursor.md) (Cursor) | **Don't spend before PMF** | Week-4 retention >20% + willingness to renew + organic word of mouth | Head of paid media at Cursor |
| [Elena Verna](../experts/elena-verna-lovable.md) (Lovable) | **Year one is a death trap** | PMF + a working organic funnel; **payback period <3 months** | ARR $400M in 18 months |
| [Meltem Berkowitz](../experts/meltem-berkowitz-deel.md) (Deel) | **Lay the foundation first** | Fast website (**<4 seconds**) → findable in search → content to catch the demand | 0→$300M in 3 years, EBITDA profitable |

### How to reconcile the four positions

**1. Matt and Elena are less opposed than they appear.**
Matt requires that before spending you get conversion tracking right and **accumulate 50 conversions** — which is itself a pre-PMF gate. What Elena opposes is "pouring money in before validating," not paid acquisition itself (asked how she'd spend an unlimited budget, the first thing she named was buying traffic at scale).

**2. Meltem is offering a sequence, not a position.**
She doesn't answer "spend or don't spend" — she gives a chain of questions you must clear in order: website → fast enough → findable in search → content to catch the demand → **only then does paid come up**.
> "If your website takes more than four seconds to load, you cannot run a successful paid advertising program."

**3. ⭐ The real consensus: paid acquisition is an amplifier, not an engine.**
All four agree on this — they just each emphasize a different component of the "engine":
- Rex emphasizes **retention** (you can't pour water into a leaky bucket)
- Matt emphasizes **tracking** (distorted signals mean the algorithm targets people at random)
- Meltem emphasizes **follow-through** (a site and content that can't catch the traffic)
- Elena emphasizes **payback period** (if you can't do the math, don't start)

**4. Practical advice**: treat these four as a single **pre-launch checklist**, not a pick-one-of-four. If any one of them is unmet, your ad spend will be systematically wasted.

## Rex Gelb's (Cursor) Decision Framework

> "About 90% of Facebook ads fail when they first launch. So the question isn't 'why isn't this ad working' — it's 'have I built a system that can quickly find what does work.'"

### How the negative feedback loop forms

Run ads without PMF →
Acquired users churn quickly →
The algorithm learns the traits of these "churned users" →
It finds you more people just like them →
The more you spend, the worse it gets

### Signals you must have before entering

1. **High retention**: Week-4 user retention > 20% (otherwise it's a "leaky bucket")
2. **Willingness to renew**: users are willing to renew; otherwise continued acquisition is itself a money-losing model
3. **Organic word of mouth**: real user referrals without heavy discounting, with conversions coming through organic traffic / referrals / basic sales

### The exception: small-scale early testing

- Budget: $2,000–$5,000
- Purpose: user recruitment, demand validation, audience-targeting tests
- **The core goal is not revenue — it's fast learning**

---

## Spend Strategy by Stage

| Stage | Monthly budget | Core goal |
|------|--------|---------|
| Seed / pre-PMF validation | $0–$20,000 | Validate that paid can work; collect signals |
| Series A (after initial PMF) | $20,000–$100,000+ | Prove out the model; test 3–4 ad types |
| Series B and beyond | $200,000+ | Scale and replicate what works |

**Note**: results take 2–3 months to stabilize; giving up too early is a common mistake.

## Matt Swulinski (Wispr Flow): Start Immediately — But Clear the Tracking Gate First

→ Full perspective in [experts/matt-swulinski-wispr-flow.md](../experts/matt-swulinski-wispr-flow.md)

**His claim**: "At the end of the day, paid acquisition is the fastest way to validate whether this product of yours can scale."

He's not saying skip content — those things are just too slow (writing articles, going on podcasts, waiting for a response); **on the paid side you can test messaging, creative, funnel, and positioning all in one week**.

**But the bar he sets for himself**:
- First get conversion tracking working and **accumulate 50 conversions** (the minimum for the algorithm to recognize your target customer)
- Give it **2–3 weeks to see direction, 3 months to see results**
> "Within two to three weeks you can tell: can I start printing money today, or do I have to go back and redo all of this."

**⚠️ He warns that 90% of companies die at this step**:
> "Say your conversion tracking is bad… your match rate is only half. I go optimize for the subscribe action, but Meta has no idea who those people are. So it targets people at random, and your acquisition cost goes through the roof. Then you say, paid doesn't work for me. **Most of the time it's not that paid doesn't work — it's that you never got the setup right before saying that.**"

**His metric conventions (two counterintuitive ones)**:
- **Early on, only look at CAC** — "leave retention to the product team, as long as the users you bring in are high quality"
- **An early LTV:CAC of 1:1 is fine** — "what you want is to get as many people using your product as fast as possible. Just don't go lower than that — lower means you're burning money"

## Elena Verna (Lovable): Year One Is a Death Trap

→ Full perspective in [experts/elena-verna-lovable.md](../experts/elena-verna-lovable.md)

- Before PMF is thoroughly validated and an organic growth funnel is working, paid spend **is just burning money**
- **Any company under five years old talking about LTV is talking nonsense** — you can only look at **payback period**
- **⭐ If your conversion cycle is longer than 3 months, don't touch paid acquisition**
- Distribution splits into **earned** and **paid** — **only earned distribution builds a moat**

---

## Meltem Berkowitz (Deel): Lay the Foundation First

→ Full perspective in [experts/meltem-berkowitz-deel.md](../experts/meltem-berkowitz-deel.md)

**Her sequence** (given when asked "how to prioritize resources early on"):
> Do you have a website? Is it fast? Do search engines know it exists? → Can people find it? If not, write content first → **only after you've answered all of these questions** should you consider paid advertising.

**Hard gate**: **if your website takes more than 4 seconds to load, you cannot run successful paid ads**.

**She also argues against early-stage B2B brand-awareness advertising**:
> "Before you start doing awareness advertising, you probably need **at least 6 to 8 months** to penetrate the bottom of the funnel — the people who are ready to convert today."

Her reasoning: awareness campaigns take time and creative, and don't necessarily work — "you've already spent a whole month and it still hasn't resonated."

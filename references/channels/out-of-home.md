# Out-of-Home (OOH) — a Placement Guide for B2B Tech Companies

**Why this channel deserves its own doc**: 2024–2026 saw a clear OOH revival among Bay Area tech companies, and several have publicly shared **complete budget breakdowns and attribution methods**. It is one of the few traditional channels with "an unusually high density of primary-source material".

**Core primary sources**:

- **Austin Hughes (CEO, Unify)** — [The Pipeline, "How to run your first OOH campaign"](https://bookthepipeline.com/p/how-to-run-your-first-ooh-campaign) (**the most transparent SF OOH budget breakdown anywhere**)

## ⭐ 1. When to Invest (two complementary frameworks)

### Reed's four hard gates (B2B SaaS)
> "if the following traits match your B2B company, **it's a no-brainer** in my mind:
> - There's a **specific location where a large number of your buyers are located** (for us, this is SF with software engineers)
> - Your **average contract value (ACV) is anywhere from mid-5 to 6-figures+** (allows you to pay back the campaign with just a few attributed deals)
> - You're a **Series B+ company**
> - You have a **large total addressable market**"

### ⚠️ Meltem's "not too early" (complements the above; no contradiction)
→ Full view: [experts/meltem-berkowitz-deel.md](../experts/meltem-berkowitz-deel.md)

> "I'm not a huge fan of early awareness campaigns for B2B businesses specifically... it'll probably take you **six to eight months minimum to tap into that bottom of the funnel** of people that are ready to convert today before you have to start doing awareness ads."

**She cites Notion as a positive example — and the reason is timing**:
> "I've seen **Notion do a great job with their out-of-home ads. They didn't do it early on, they started doing it much later in their journey** by the time when everybody that was working in tech knew what it was, and it was really to **drive home the message continuously rather than to introduce themselves to the world**."
>
> "the ads that I'm recalling right now were **showcasing their product interface**... **Notion has a very specific interface that when you see it, you recognize it**... **If they did that day one, people would be like, 'What is this thing that I'm looking at?'**"

**→ The two frameworks combined**: OOH is a channel for **reinforcing memory**, not for **introducing yourself**. First exhaust the bottom of the funnel (6–8 months); only once your target audience already recognizes you can OOH work.

### Theoretical basis: the 95:5 rule
(Ehrenberg-Bass / John Dawes) At any given moment, only about **5%** of potential buyers are in-market.

Reed's phrasing:
> "only a fraction of your ideal customer profile is actually in the market right now. An OOH campaign aims to **make your audience feel something now so that they remember your brand in the future**."

## 2. ⭐ The Stytch Case (including one widely circulated error)

### ⚠️ First, correcting a common misretelling

The version circulating online goes: "Stytch mapped out the **most common route** its ICP commutes into San Francisco, and put billboards **only along that route**."

**That is inaccurate.** Reed's original words:
> "For that campaign, we bought **80 bus ads, 130 bus and transit shelter ads, eight billboards on SF city streets, and, yes, three billboards on Highway 101.**"

**= a citywide saturation buy of 221 ad units**, not "one route only". Commute-route mapping was just **one of the site-selection methods**:
> "For out-of-home, it's not enough to know who your ideal buyer is; you have to **understand their physical habits—where they live, where they work, and how they move back and forth**... We **mapped our target Sales accounts, typical commuting routes, and conference venues**, then picked ad locations to maximize impressions near those locations."

### Results data (April 2024 campaign, SF, one month)

> "During the campaign, we saw a **more than 25% uplift in branded search traffic** and an **over 70% increase in branded search clicks globally** (social media is powerful!). We also **sustained a 10% to 15% uplift after the billboards came down**."
>
> "Further down the funnel, **more than 10% of our sales opportunities created since the campaign have explicitly cited billboards or bus ads as a factor in exploring Stytch**."

**⚠️ The attribution method must be flagged**: that 10% is **self-reported attribution** — the prospect volunteered the mention when the sales opportunity was created; **no control group, no experimental design**. Reed himself admits:
> "attribution for physical ads is **murky and imperfect**. **You may never get a big final number to share in your board presentation, and that's OK.**"

**⚠️ Citation traps**:
- Secondhand retellings routinely **cross wires**: pinning the 2022 campaign's numbers (83%/170%/181%/257%, from agency Division of Labor's self-promotional page) onto 2024
- One newsletter **swapped** the 25% and 70% figures
- Reed's LinkedIn post **contains no numbers at all**, only qualitative decision criteria — when citing numbers, always cite the Lenny's Newsletter original
- Additional fact: Stytch was acquired by **Twilio** in October 2025

## ⭐ 3. Budget Scale (Unify's complete breakdown, SF)

**$100K / 6 weeks / 4 unit types / 1 city (September 2025)**

| Unit | Count | Spend |
|------|------|------|
| Bus wraps | 10 | $21K |
| Pillars | 6/week × 4 weeks = 24 | $26K |
| Wallscape | 1 | $21K |
| Billboards | 3 | $32K |

**⭐ Three hard conclusions**:
> - **"$100K is table stakes for major markets"** — "For a market like San Francisco, $100K was the amount we felt we needed to see any results or signal. **$50K would've been a sunk cost.**"
> - **"One billboard = zero impact. You need critical mass."** (3+ exposures are needed to form memory)
> - **3-month lead time**: "if you want to hit a key event window, **start at least 3 months out**"

**Go through an agency, not direct**: via Quan Media Group, Unify got units listed at $130K+ for under $100K.

### SF market price quick reference

| Item | Amount | Source |
|------|------|------|
| Effective entry threshold (single city) | **$100K** (<$50K is basically a sunk cost) | Unify (primary) |
| Highway 101 flagship locations | $20K–$60K+/month | AdQuick (⚠️ vendor) |
| Top-tier 101 locations | **$200K–$250K/month** (roughly $3M annualized) | SF Standard, citing industry estimates |
| Bay Area highway bulletin exposure | 80K–250K daily impressions | AdQuick (⚠️ vendor) |
| Flagship location booking lead time | 12–16 weeks | AdQuick (⚠️ vendor) |

**The 101-corridor phenomenon** (SF Standard investigative report, 2026-08): the biggest, most visible boards have **risen 20%–30% in price** since before the pandemic; 100K+ people cross the Bay Bridge into the city daily; there are 200+ billboards between SFO and San Jose.

## ⭐ 4. Attribution Methods (ranked by reliability)

This is the most practical part of this doc — **if you're doing OOH, measurement must be built in advance**; otherwise all you'll get afterward is "murky" numbers.

| # | Method | Who uses it | Reliability |
|---|------|--------|--------|
| 1 | **Control-group experiments** (matched exposed vs control populations to measure conversion lift) | Unify (through their agency) | Strongest |
| 2 | **⭐ Sales call recording scans** | **Clay** | High and actionable |
| 3 | Self-reported attribution ("how did you hear about us") | Stytch's 10% | Medium |
| 4 | Branded search volume (split into during-campaign / post-campaign windows) | Stytch's 25%/70%/10–15% | Medium |
| 5 | Geo-based traffic and meetings booked | Unify | Medium |
| 6 | Mobile location data (Placer.ai and the like) | — | Fits B2C offline foot traffic; limited meaning for B2B |
| 7 | Brand lift surveys (Geopath / MFour / OAAA) | — | Better suited to big-budget B2C |

**⭐ Clay's approach is the one most worth copying** (Bruno Estrella, verbatim):
> "It's traditionally been challenging for companies to track the impact of something like an ad at a busy San Francisco intersection. But we've figured that out, too: the team **scans Gong call recordings for mentions of the billboards, pulls that into Clay, and ties it back to pipeline**."

**Clay's site-selection method is also hardcore**:
> "We **mapped where our target accounts are located in San Francisco** (literally have a huge map with pins on it, **using Clay** of course) and Jessica and I **flew to San Francisco to evaluate every single billboard and its visibility**."
>
> On timing: **"September and October → Inbound, Sculpt, Dreamforce, and a16z Startup week are all happening."**

## ⭐ 5. Creative and Amplification (social media is the real battleground)

**The memory formula** (Kira Klaas, Notion):
> "Effective OOH is about **frequency** (how often it's seen) and **resonance** ('this feels relevant to me'), both of which help with **recall**."

**⭐ Physical exposure is just the opener; social amplification is the real battleground**:
- Unify: "**a ton of the impressions and value will come from social, not physical views**"
- **Ramp** (Cody Morgan, VP Demand Gen):
  > "The best tip I can give someone planning to invest in OOH for the first time is to **get loud on social media when the campaign goes live**. **Hire a professional photographer** to take photos of the placements to make it easy for team members, investors, and friends and family to post on social media."
- Stytch ran an internal `#billboard-spotting` Slack channel; employees posted an average of 2.1 photos each

**The logic of mixing unit types** (Kasper Koczab, head of OOH at Brex and the operating advisor on the Stytch campaign):
> "we aim to have a good balance of **high-profile units** (usually units that people literally have to look up to see), along with a format that offers a **wider footprint** (street furniture or transit) in order to deliver frequency. **While folks might only recall the big unit on the sales call, the smaller formats that deliver frequency play a crucial role in driving recall.**"

**⭐ "Most people won't get it" is a feature, not a bug** (Division of Labor, the creative agency on the Stytch campaign):
> "Yes, it is good that the majority of people don't understand your advertising. **As long as the people you want to talk to understand.** In fact, when most people don't understand your messages that's even better for the people that do understand, because they **feel part of something special**."

**Creative standard** (Sarah Scharf, CMO, Vanta):
> "The most important thing is a **simple and compelling creative concept—ideally one that elicits an emotional response**. It doesn't have to be humor (as we typically lean into), but it has to be **easily understood and memorable**."

**Never skip the site survey**: Unify checked every location's angles and traffic flow on Google Street View and then walked the sites in person; Clay's team flew straight to SF to assess every board's visibility one by one.

## ⭐ 7. ABM × OOH: OOH as an Account-Level Weapon

**What Stytch and Clay did is, at its core, ABM + OOH** — both first **pinned their target sales accounts' physical locations onto a map**, then chose units accordingly.

**Four actionable plays** (source: OneScreen.ai webinar — ⚠️ vendor content; the methods are worth referencing, but discount the case data):

1. **Break digital fatigue**: B2B buyers expect to be targeted online; **they don't expect to see you outside their office building**. The surprise itself is an advantage.

2. **⭐ Not just top-of-funnel — use it to advance deals in flight**: when only one stakeholder is engaged on a deal, run placements around their HQ or at conferences their decision-makers will attend, to **reach the entire buying committee**.

3. **Data-driven site selection**: layer geographic data + mobile device activity + demographics to locate where decision-makers live, work, and gather.

4. **Digital + physical + experiential, three layers linked**:
   - A billboard next to the target account's HQ + direct mail carrying the same message to every member of the buying committee + digital ads with the same creative (the Sendoso play)
   - **An LED truck parked outside the target account's door, swapping creative to match whichever customer you're meeting that day**
   - A themed billboard + a pop-up next to it handing out cold brew

**Budget reality check** (Austin Sandmeyer, Sendoso): most teams spend more than they realize on underperforming LinkedIn and display; measured by "cost per real impression" or "impact on deal velocity", OOH often comes out cheaper. Beyond big boards there are lower-barrier options: digital screens, LED trucks, elevator screens, transit ads.

## 8. The Core B2B vs B2C Differences

| Dimension | B2B | B2C |
|------|-----|-----|
| Theoretical basis | **The 95:5 rule** — only 5% of potential buyers are in-market at any moment | Short conversion windows; can pay off near-term |
| Goal | Build **mental availability** so buyers think of you when they enter the market | Directly drive purchase / store visits |
| Measurement | Self-reported attribution + branded search + sales calls; **lags by months** | Foot-traffic lift, sales lift, geo-based controls possible |
| Placement logic | **Commute routes + target accounts' offices + conference venues** | High-footfall commercial districts |
| Payback logic | **High ACV; a few deals pay it back** | Volume-driven |
